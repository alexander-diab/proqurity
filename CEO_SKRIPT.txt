================================================================================
BEFUND - WHAT TO SAY TO A CEO
Spoken script, about three minutes. Plain language, no jargon.
Read the notes in [square brackets] - do not say them out loud.
================================================================================


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0:00  THE PROBLEM, IN ONE BREATH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"Every large company already knows WHAT happened in its purchasing.
 The systems record all of it.

 What no system can tell you is whether it was ALLOWED.

 Because the reason is never in the system. It is in the contract,
 and it is in somebody's email."


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0:30  MAKE IT CONCRETE - ONE REAL CASE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[This is a real case from our data. Every number below is checkable.]

"Let me show you one order.

 We buy a chemical - titanium dioxide - for one hundred and thirty-four
 thousand euros. Standard purchase. Nothing unusual.

 A month after we place the order, the supplier raises the price.
 Our system records that. It does not record why, and it does not
 record whether they were allowed to.

 So we went and looked. Three things came back.

 First: our contract with that supplier says a price rise must be
 announced thirty days in advance. They gave us three.

 Second: the contract allows small adjustments without notice -
 up to three percent. This one was fourteen point six percent.

 Third - and this is the one that surprised us - the person inside
 OUR company who entered that price change is authorised to approve
 up to one hundred thousand euros. The order was worth a hundred
 and thirty-four thousand.

 Three separate control failures. On one order. And every one of them
 was invisible to every system we already had."


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1:30  WHAT IT DOES AT SCALE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"That was one order. We ran it across the whole set.

 Three hundred and nineteen orders had a price change worth looking at.

 Of those - seventy-nine were fine. Properly announced, notice given,
 rule followed. We can prove it and move on.

 Forty-four we simply cannot explain. There is no email, no document,
 no trace of anyone agreeing to it. That is not an accusation. It is
 a list of things somebody should ask about.

 Thirty-four are suspected breaches of contract. The document exists,
 we read it, and it contradicts what the contract requires.

 And the rest - a hundred and sixty-two - we deliberately refuse to
 judge, because there is no framework contract with that supplier at
 all. There is no rule to measure them against."

[Pause here. The refusal to judge is the credibility moment.
 Anyone who has been sold an AI tool expects it to answer everything.]


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2:15  WHY YOU CAN TRUST THE NUMBER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"Now, the obvious question. Why would you believe any of this?

 So we checked it. Every one of the three hundred and nineteen cases
 had a known correct answer, written down before the system existed.

 The system got three hundred and nineteen out of three hundred and
 nineteen right.

 And here is the important part about HOW.

 The system does not guess numbers. It does not let the AI decide
 whether a rule was broken. Every date, every euro, every percentage
 is read straight out of the contract and the email and the record.
 The arithmetic is arithmetic.

 The AI does exactly one job: it writes the paragraph at the bottom
 that explains the finding to a human being. It is forbidden from
 introducing a single number of its own.

 That is deliberate. In an audit, a number nobody can trace back to
 a document is worse than no number at all."


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2:45  WHAT I HAVE TO BE HONEST ABOUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

[Say this. Do not let someone else find it.]

"One thing I have to put on the table.

 The purchase records are real - a published data set from a real
 chemical company. The contracts and emails are not. We generated
 them, because no company publishes its contracts.

 So the perfect score proves the machinery works end to end.
 It does not yet prove it works on your messy real documents.
 That is the next test, and it needs your data.

 There are also two checks we report but cannot yet prove - the
 percentage tolerance and the approval limits. They are on the
 report, clearly marked as unproven."


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3:00  THE CLOSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"Here is the shape of it.

 Today, checking one of these takes a person about twenty minutes.
 Find the order, find the contract, search the mailbox, decide.

 This does it in under a second, and it hands you a one-page report
 with every claim linked back to the document it came from.

 Purchasing is just where we started. The pattern is everywhere:
 any process that leaves its record in one system and its reasons
 in documents has the same blind spot between the two.

 We are not building a report. We are building the join."


================================================================================
IF THEY ASK
================================================================================

"How is this different from what we already have?"
   Your current tools find the unusual thing. They cannot open the
   contract and tell you whether the unusual thing was permitted.
   That judgement is the whole product.

"Why not just use ChatGPT on our documents?"
   Because searching by similarity finds the wrong document.
   We measured it: asked how much notice was given on one order,
   a plain document search confidently returned a DIFFERENT
   supplier's price letter - and rated it a better match than the
   correct one. All price letters look alike. They differ only in
   the order number and the date, which is exactly what that kind
   of search is worst at. Our system follows the actual link from
   the order to its own documents, so it cannot pick up the wrong one.

"What would it cost to run?"
   The expensive step runs once per finding, not once per order.
   A quarter of a million order lines becomes a few hundred cases
   worth a second look.

"How long to try it on our data?"
   The purchasing records load in an afternoon. The real work is
   your contracts, and that is a conversation about which twenty
   contracts cover eighty percent of spend.

"What happens when it is wrong?"
   Every finding is a claim with the document attached. A person
   opens the email and disagrees in ten seconds. It is built to be
   checked, not believed.
